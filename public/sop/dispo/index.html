15 minutes · one named owner · before any document is pulled

02

Pull the four documents

30 minutes · four pulls, exact specs · this is where the most time gets lost

Four documents build the packet. Nearly every rebuild traces back to a bad pull here — a stale address left in a search box, a map zoomed too far out, an export that stopped short of the legend. Pull them right once.

1 FEMA FIRMette — msc.fema.gov

  • Clear the search box first. Delete whatever address is sitting in it. The site keeps the last one and will happily hand you a map of someone else's lot.
  • Type the subject address. Nothing else.
  • Export the FIRMette. Check the community name printed at the top before you save.

2 USFWS Wetlands (NWI) — fwsprimary.wim.usgs.gov

  • Zoom in until the scale bar reads between 1:1,500 and 1:3,500. That is lot scale.
  • A regional sheet at 1:7,000 or wider cannot clear a single lot — it is a picture of the neighborhood, and it is worthless to a buyer's engineer.
  • The subject address must print on the sheet.

3 USDA Web Soil Survey — websoilsurvey.nrcs.usda.gov

  • Draw the AOI box on the parcel itself, not on the general area.
  • The export must include the Map Unit Legend. If the footer says "Page 1 of 3," you need all three pages.
  • Without the legend you have a soil symbol and no soil name — which means the packet cannot state what the dirt is.

4 Regrid parcel screenshot — regrid.com

  • Full browser window, maximum zoom at which the whole lot still fits.
  • Red boundary visible. The road visible. The neighboring lots visible — that is how the utilities call gets made in Phase 3.
  • Do not crop to the lot alone. Context is the point.

03

Make the utilities call

10 minutes · the single most damaging line in any packet

"No utilities" is not a finding. It is a description of every vacant lot in America. Nothing is connected to any empty parcel. But a developer reads "no utilities" as no service available in this area — and that either kills the deal outright or kills your credibility the moment he drives past a neighbor's running pool pump.

Open the Regrid aerial. Look at the block, not the lot. Then pick one of three answers for each of electric, water, sewer.

If you see
Available at Street

Occupied homes on the same block — roofs, driveways, pool cages, parked cars. Service is in the street. The buyer pays a connection, not a line extension.

If you see
Verify In Street

Platted subdivision, mostly vacant woods, a few scattered homes. Lines are probably in but unconfirmed. Say exactly that, and say it is unconfirmed.

If you see
Service Area Not Reached

Raw acreage, no homes in sight, no poles along the road. This is well-and-septic territory. Now "no utilities" is true — and you can defend it.

Gate 3 — Utilities reconciled0 / 4
If held — call the county utility or the power co-op. Ten minutes on the phone beats a developer catching it.

Fix this upstream too

The acquisitions intake sheet should force the same three-way choice instead of a free-text utilities box. "Available at street" and "service area not reached" are different facts about a property, and right now both arrive as "no utilities."

04

Build and clear the packet

Same day · Template A, pre-contract · nothing ships with an open flag

Submit the four documents, the reconciled utilities answers and the eight intake fields. The packet comes back as one merged PDF. Then read the flags.

▸ Resolve every flag

Any row tagged Verify, Report Pending or Read This is an open item. Two choices, no third:

  • Re-pull and rebuild — when the document was the problem.
  • Market with the flag disclosed in writing — when the fact is the problem. An unmapped FEMA panel or a landlocked parcel is still sellable. Hiding it is not.
Gate 4 — Release check0 / 7
If held — it does not go out. A packet with an unblurred owner name or a stray asking price cannot be recalled once it is in fifty inboxes.
05

Run the cascade

72-hour clock · four tiers · every touch logged

The cascade exists for two reasons: your best buyers get a real window before the lot is everywhere, and you stop burning the full list on every deal. Start the clock when the packet clears Gate 4.

T+0
→2 hr
Tier 1 — Matched buyers
Pull from Vetted Buyers. Filter on county, acreage and intended use. Call first, then text, then email the packet. Every matched name touched inside two hours. These are the people who buy without a price tag because they already trust the pipeline.
T+4 hr
Tier 2 — Market list
Everyone on the buyer list in that state or metro. One email, packet attached. Send through the CRM or BCC — never a visible to-line with fifty buyers in it.
T+24 hr
Tier 3 — Agents & developers
Land brokers, builders and developers working that county. Same packet, different ask: "bring me your buyer." An agent with a buyer is worth more than ten cold contacts.
T+48 hr
Tier 4 — Open market
Facebook land groups, Land.com / Lands of America, LandWatch, Craigslist. Public post, still no price. Packet on request so you capture the contact.
T+72 hr
Huddle — do not just repost
Zero offers at 72 hours is information. Check three things in order: is the utilities line scaring people off, is an open flag doing it, and is the seller's number even reachable for this county. Reposting a deal that is priced wrong just teaches the list to ignore you.

Log every touch — this is the part that is unorganized right now

Buyer name · channel · timestamp · response. Four fields. With two managers working live deals, the log is the only thing standing between you and a buyer getting the same lot pitched twice in an afternoon.

Gate 5 — Cascade complete0 / 4
If held — finish the tier you skipped before adding another channel. Breadth without the calls is just noise.

Standing terms — not negotiable at dispo level

  1. No asking price is published. Buyers submit what they can pay and how they want to structure it.
  2. Freedom Offers controls title and escrow. The buyer does not select the title company.
  3. Buyer pays all closing costs.
  4. All offers and deal questions route to Sharyn and Marie:
